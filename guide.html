FIELD NOTE 001 · ROLE DESIGN

Define the role
before you hire.

Turn “we need help” into a role a remote teammate can understand and your team can evaluate.

THE QUICK VERSION

Write down the work, the working pattern, the tools, and what a good first month should produce.

Role planning · Updated 19 October 2026
IN THIS GUIDE01   Start with the work02   Describe the pattern03   Define outcomes04   Use the checklistSources

A useful role brief gives the candidate, interviewer, and manager the same picture of the job. Begin with four decisions: what the person will own, when they need to collaborate, which tools they will use, and how the first month will be assessed.

01 / THE WORK

Start with the work, not the title.

List the recurring tasks you want someone to take on. For each task, write the output you need and who currently makes the final decision. This separates day-to-day ownership from decisions that stay with a manager or specialist.

Describe one typical week. Which requests arrive predictably? Which need judgment? What information or access does the person need to finish the task? Answers to those questions usually make a role more concrete than a long list of traits.

EXAMPLE BRIEF LINE

“Prepare the weekly customer support summary from help desk records; the team lead reviews trends and decides on policy changes.”

02 / THE WORKING PATTERN

Make collaboration visible.

State the hours or overlap your team needs, the channels used for routine work, and which tasks can be completed asynchronously. If the role needs live coverage, specify the window and time zone instead of saying “flexible hours.”

The International Labour Organization recommends clear expectations about results, contactable hours, and how progress will be discussed for telework. It also highlights access to appropriate tools and training. Read the ILO guidance.

03 / THE OUTCOMES

Describe a good first month.

Choose a few observable outcomes that fit the role. They might include a documented workflow, a completed handover, or an agreed service rhythm. Keep the measures connected to the work rather than activity for its own sake.

Write down who will review the work and when feedback will happen. A first-month plan does not need to predict everything; it gives both sides a way to recognize progress and adjust expectations.

04 / YOUR CHECKLIST

Before you post the role

  • ✓ Name the recurring tasks and expected outputs.
  • ✓ Clarify decision boundaries and who reviews the work.
  • ✓ State working hours, time zone overlap, and response expectations.
  • ✓ List the tools and access needed for the job.
  • ✓ Define a few first-month outcomes you can discuss together.
SOURCE NOTE

Further reading

International Labour Organization, “Keys for effective teleworking.” Published in 2020 during the pandemic; its points on clear expectations, tools, and results-based management inform this guide. The worksheet and examples here are editorial suggestions, not ILO instructions.
