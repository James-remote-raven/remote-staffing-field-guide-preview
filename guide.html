FIELD NOTE 001 · ROLE DESIGN

Define the role
before you hire.

A short planning worksheet for turning “we need help” into a role a remote teammate can understand and your team can evaluate.

THE QUICK VERSION

Write down the work, the working pattern, the tools, and what a good first month should produce.

5-minute read · Preview edition

A useful role brief gives a candidate, interviewer, and manager the same picture of the job. You can start with four decisions: what the person will own, when they need to collaborate, which tools they will use, and how the first month will be assessed.

01 / THE WORK

Start with the work, not the title.

List the recurring tasks you want someone to take on. For each task, write the output you need and who currently makes the final decision. This helps separate day-to-day ownership from decisions that should stay with a manager or specialist.

TRY THIS

“Prepare the weekly customer support summary from our help desk records; the team lead reviews trends and chooses any policy changes.”

02 / THE WORKING PATTERN

Make collaboration visible.

State the hours or overlap your team needs, the channels used for routine work, and which tasks can be completed asynchronously. If a role needs live coverage, specify the window and time zone instead of saying “flexible hours.”

03 / THE OUTCOMES

Describe a good first month.

Choose a few observable outcomes that fit the role. They might include a documented workflow, a completed handover, or an agreed service rhythm. Keep the measures connected to the work rather than activity for its own sake.

04 / YOUR CHECKLIST

Before you post the role

  • ✓ Name the recurring tasks and expected outputs.
  • ✓ Clarify decision boundaries and who reviews the work.
  • ✓ State working hours, time zone overlap, and response expectations.
  • ✓ List the tools and access needed for the job.
  • ✓ Define a few first-month outcomes you can discuss together.
