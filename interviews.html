FIELD NOTE 002 · CANDIDATE SELECTION

Interview for
the work.

A fair comparison starts with the same job-related questions and a clear idea of what a strong answer looks like.

THE QUICK VERSION

Choose the skills that matter, ask comparable questions, and score answers against the role.

Candidate selection · Updated 19 October 2026
IN THIS GUIDE01   Pick the criteria02   Ask comparable questions03   Record evidence04   Use the checklistSources

An interview should help you decide whether someone can do the work you defined. A structured approach keeps the conversation useful for both sides and makes later comparisons easier to explain.

01 / THE CRITERIA

Start from the role brief.

Choose a small number of job-related capabilities that will matter in the first few months. For a remote coordinator, that could include organizing incoming requests, communicating status, and spotting when a decision needs escalation. Keep each criterion tied to an actual task.

Before interviewing, write what a basic, capable, and strong response would demonstrate. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management describes structured interviews as a way to assess job-related competencies with predetermined questions and common rating standards. Read OPM’s overview.

02 / THE QUESTIONS

Ask about real situations.

Use the same core questions for every candidate. Ask for an example of past work or give a realistic situation related to the role. Follow-up questions can clarify an answer, but keep the main prompt and evaluation criteria consistent.

EXAMPLE QUESTION

“Two colleagues send urgent requests at the same time, and you cannot finish both today. What would you do first, and how would you keep each person informed?”

The example above tests prioritization and communication. It is not a complete assessment on its own. Adapt questions to the actual role and check local hiring requirements where you operate.

03 / THE NOTES

Record what you heard.

Write brief notes against each criterion while the answer is fresh. Separate the candidate’s example from your impression of their style. A simple rating scale can help, provided you define what each level means before the interview.

OPM’s guidance uses the same questions and standards for candidates in a structured interview. Its guidance is written for U.S. federal hiring, so treat it as a method reference rather than a rule for your jurisdiction. See the source.

04 / YOUR CHECKLIST

Before the first interview

  • ✓ Choose job-related capabilities from the role brief.
  • ✓ Write the same core questions for all candidates.
  • ✓ Define what the rating scale means.
  • ✓ Give interviewers a place to record examples.
  • ✓ Compare evidence against the role, not only general impressions.
SOURCE NOTE

Further reading

U.S. Office of Personnel Management, “Structured Interviews.” The source explains predetermined questions, job-related competencies, and common rating standards. The example question and checklist here are editorial suggestions for remote roles.
