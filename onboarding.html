FIELD NOTE 003 · ONBOARDING

Plan the
first month.

A new remote teammate needs a clear path into the work, the people, and the systems—not a calendar full of introductions.

THE QUICK VERSION

Prepare access, name a point of contact, agree on early work, and schedule feedback.

Onboarding · Updated 19 October 2026
IN THIS GUIDE01   Before day one02   The first week03   The first month04   Use the checklistSources

Remote onboarding becomes easier to follow when practical setup and work expectations are written down. The plan below is a starting point; adapt it to the role, location, and level of access involved.

01 / BEFORE DAY ONE

Prepare the essentials.

Confirm the equipment, accounts, and documents the person needs for their first tasks. Decide who grants access, who can solve a technical problem, and which systems should remain off-limits until required. Test the first login path before the start date.

NIST’s enterprise telework guidance treats organization-issued and personal devices, remote access, and related policies as security considerations. The exact controls belong to your security team; onboarding should follow those decisions rather than improvising them. Read NIST’s guidance.

02 / THE FIRST WEEK

Show how work moves.

Give the new teammate one clear point of contact and a short map of the team: where requests arrive, where decisions are recorded, and when to ask for help. Start with a real but bounded task so both sides can see the workflow in action.

EXAMPLE FIRST TASK

“Review three completed support tickets, summarize the handoff steps you observe, and note any part of the process that is unclear.”

The International Labour Organization emphasizes appropriate tools, training, clear expectations, and contact with colleagues for telework. Those points make a useful check on whether your first-week plan covers more than account setup. Read the ILO guidance.

03 / THE FIRST MONTH

Review the work together.

Set a few first-month outcomes and a regular time to discuss progress. Ask what is working, where information is missing, and whether the original role brief still matches the work. Make adjustments visible to everyone who depends on the role.

For distributed teams, it helps to be explicit about contactable hours and how progress will be discussed. The ILO’s telework guidance recommends clear expectations about results, hours, and reporting without overly burdensome monitoring. See the source.

04 / YOUR CHECKLIST

Make the start plan concrete

  • ✓ Confirm the first tasks and the accounts they require.
  • ✓ Name a manager, a day-to-day contact, and a technical support route.
  • ✓ Document working hours and communication channels.
  • ✓ Schedule the first review of work and expectations.
  • ✓ Ask the new teammate what remains unclear.
SOURCE NOTES

Further reading

International Labour Organization, “Keys for effective teleworking.” Published in 2020; supports the points about tools, training, contact, and clear expectations.

NIST SP 800-46 Rev. 2, “Guide to Enterprise Telework, Remote Access, and Bring Your Own Device Security.” Published in 2016; supports the need to handle remote access and device security deliberately. This guide does not prescribe a specific technical setup.
